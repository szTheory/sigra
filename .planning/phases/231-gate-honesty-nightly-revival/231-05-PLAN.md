<objective>
D-11 step 3, made a first-class task instead of a checkbox: **run the harness in CI and read what
happens.** The eval harness's phases b1 through b6 — the stale-render guard, the evidence-anchor
integrity check, the fix-queue derived-field lint, the quality-findings monotonic guard, the award
ledger verify-then-climb guard, and the settled-findings lint — have never once executed in CI,
because the job has failed inside its Playwright phase every sampled time. Their pass is therefore
unproven, and GATE-04's requirement text is explicit that they must *demonstrably execute*.

RESEARCH enumerated six independent precondition classes (R4 through R9) that can red a b-phase in
CI but not locally, and rated one of them near-certain. Phase (a2)'s `fix-queue-build.mjs` is the
sole writer of `open_findings` and rewrites it in the working tree; b3, b4 and b5 then compare that
working tree against **committed HEAD**. The committed ledger holds 33,642 findings across 186 cells,
captured on some earlier, possibly partial render. An ubuntu-CI-native render will differ per cell,
and `quality-findings-monotonic.sh` defaults an absent-at-base cell to zero — so any new cell with a
non-zero count fails unconditionally. That is not a bug in the guard; it is the guard doing its job
against a baseline captured somewhere else.

The remedy is the Phase 219 move: regenerate at CI-native truth and commit the result as its own
reviewed change, so the forward-only guard compares CI-native to CI-native from then on. But that
re-bases the baseline every later phase climbs against, which is a door that does not swing both
ways — hence the decision checkpoint before it. The alternative is real and must be considered
honestly: where a count genuinely ROSE, that is a regression and belongs in the fix queue, not in a
new baseline.

Purpose: find out what b1-b6 actually do in CI, and put the eval lane on a truthful baseline.
Output: a read run with its banners quoted, a recorded decision, and — if approved — CI-native
ledgers committed as their own change.
</objective>
