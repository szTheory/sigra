It is derived from the pressure-test-audit-v2.md Section 8 REWORK verdict. Phase 179 reads this file as its primary input; no other pre-reading is required to begin candidate generation.

    SECTION: BRIEF PROVENANCE
    One-paragraph back-reference: The logo system REWORK verdict in brandbook/pressure-test-audit-v2.md Section 8 identified the primary failure mode (mark-beside-text is the generic lockup in the Elixir ecosystem) and directed the creation of this brief. The audit's Section 13 action plan places Phase 179 exploration as the immediate next step. This brief commits the constraints so Phase 179 does not re-open scope.

    SECTION: HARD CONSTRAINTS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
    Seven numbered constraints with bold labels. Use the exact text from CONTEXT.md logo v2 design brief inputs, cast into the brief's constraint format:
    1. **No rectangular background behind the logomark.** The mark must work on transparent and colored surfaces without a rectangular or rounded-rectangle container behind it; boundary-breaking compositions are encouraged.
    2. **Logotype sits appropriately close to the logomark.** In combination lockups, the wordmark is visually adjacent to the mark — not separated by whitespace that makes them feel like two independent elements.
    3. **Primary lockup carries no subtitle or slogan.** The main combination lockup is mark+wordmark only. A separate with-subtitle variant is a required deliverable but is a distinct design problem, not an afterthought.
    4. **Integrated typemark variants are required.** At least 2 of the 5–7 candidates must be fully integrated typemarks: the rail/brand motif is worked into the letterforms structurally (crossbar, counter, stroke terminal, descender) — not placed beside the type as a separate glyph.
    5. **The primary lockup is not mark-beside-text.** The mark-left-of-logotype construction (Option A Core Rails) is the v1 reference baseline; it is archived, not built upon. Candidates that are mark-beside-text are acceptable as non-typemark options only if they demonstrate markedly better integration quality than v1.
    6. **Font and color are tunable within the ember anchor.** The typeface may change from Inter Display Black if a stronger OFL candidate exists. Palette tuning means shade variation within the warm amber range (hue 15–40 degrees on the color wheel, from brick-red through amber to apricot). No candidate uses a hue outside this range. The ember-700 value (#c2410c) is the anchor; deep-ember (#9a3412) and accent-soft (#fdba74) are the tuning poles.
    7. **Options must be shown for human selection at Phase 180.** No candidate is implicitly ratified. Phase 180 is the explicit human gate. Phase 179 commits gallery only; Phase 180 records the decision.

This distinction is the palette identity to preserve and anchor Phase 179 candidate exploration.

    SECTION: SIGRA LETTERFORM INTEGRATION ANATOMY
    Opening sentence: The word "sigra" (lowercase, 5 letters: s, i, g, r, a) offers four integration points for the rail metaphor. Then a numbered list of integration points (from 178-RESEARCH.md Integrated Typemark Craft Notes): (1) g descender — the prime integration opportunity; can be shaped into a rail continuation, track curve, or signal path; the `g` descender in all candidate fonts terminates freely below the baseline. (2) i tittle — the dot above the `i` can become the ember accent dot; applied as the only colored element in an otherwise black/monochrome wordmark, it echoes the current mark's accent logic. (3) s entry and exit strokes — can echo the staggered-bar rail motif from the Rail Accent mark by giving the `s` terminal strokes a flat, horizontal cut instead of an angled finish. (4) r shoulder — the arm of the `r` can terminate in a rail-bar geometry (flat-cut or bracketed) to reinforce the motif without disrupting readability.

    Then a subsection: INTEGRATION PITFALLS TO AVOID. Bulleted list from 178-RESEARCH.md Integrated Typemark Craft Notes — Forced ligature (connecting g+r or r+a so they read as one glyph); Gimmick letter (only one letter is distinctive while the rest are plain); Stroke contrast damage (motif contrast clashes with typeface's inherent contrast level); Kerning distortion (rail insertions change optical spacing requiring manual correction); Scale fragility (motif detail disappears at 32px and below — all candidates must pass the 16/32/54px render gate before gallery presentation).

    Then a subsection: OPTICAL ADJUSTMENTS REQUIRED POST-INTEGRATION. Three-point list from 178-RESEARCH.md: (1) Check visual weight of the integrated element against surrounding letterforms. (2) Verify the silhouette reads as a coherent word at 50% opacity grayscale (the "tattoo test"). (3) Check letter spacing after motif insertion — modified letterforms typically need 5–15% tighter tracking.

    SECTION: RENDER-CRITIQUE RUBRIC
    Six-row table: Test / Pass Condition / Scale to apply. Rows: (1) Wordmark legibility — "sigra" is readable as five distinct letters — 16px, 32px, 54px, hero; (2) Integration coherence — motif reads as structural part of the letterform, not an overlay — 32px and hero; (3) Ecosystem distinctiveness — silhouette does not resemble Phoenix, Ash, Oban Pro, or Elixir Lang at glance distance — hero; (4) Ember anchor — dominant hue falls within 15–40 degree warm amber range — hero and social card; (5) Scale durability — the 16px favicon render has a readable silhouette without the wordmark — 16px favicon; (6) Tattoo test — at 50% opacity grayscale the wordmark silhouette is coherent and word-shaped — 54px.
