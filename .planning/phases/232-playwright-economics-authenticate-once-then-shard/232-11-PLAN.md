<objective>
Split the residual Playwright seams across concurrent matrix legs and put a thin, name-preserving
aggregator in front of them, so the lane's wall clock stops being the sum of its seams while the
required check keeps resolving.

Purpose: PW-02 and SC-2. After PW-01 and the design extraction, `example_playwright_smoke` still runs
four seams one after another against one boot and one database, because `playwright.config.ts:11-13`
records that DB state is shared across specs. D-15 fixes the isolation shape — per-shard Postgres and
per-shard app, not `workers > 1` against one boot — and D-21/D-22 fix the required-check shape: the
working job interpolates the matrix value into its own name, and a separate non-matrix job keeps the
byte-identical required name and reds unless every leg succeeded.
Output: `example_playwright_shard` (matrix), a thin `example_playwright_smoke` aggregator, per-leg
databases and artifact names, `--retries=0` and the non-emptiness wrapper on every concurrent
invocation, and the manifest, documentation and contracts updated with it.
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
The `assumption-delta scan` detector fired (`detected: true`). The core assumption that has lost its
monopoly is **one booted example app against one dev database** — after this plan there are N of each,
one per matrix leg plus one on the design job. The nouns that become primary are the **leg** and its
**per-leg database**, not "the app".

Decision: **promote**. The plan does not add a "second app" alongside a privileged first one; it makes
per-leg isolation the shape every Playwright job uses. Concretely: `pg-database` is a composite input
with a per-leg value at every call site rather than a special case for one job, the server-log path is
per-job, and the artifact names are per-leg. The sibling generalisation — one boot-prelude definition
with N callers — was already promoted by plans 232-06 through 232-09 when the composite replaced the
seven verbatim copies.

What would force a later promote if this were deferred instead: the first time a second job needed a
non-default database, the value would have to be threaded through anyway, and a one-off `env:` would
not work — a composite action's steps do not inherit the caller's `env` context, which is why `port`
and `pg-database` are inputs rather than job-level variables.
</assumption_delta_decision>
